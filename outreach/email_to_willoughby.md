Dear A/Prof Willoughby,

Professor [Patric Oliver](https://research.monash.edu/en/persons/patrick-olivier/) suggested I reach out. I'm an MDS student at Monash Malaysia and a late-deafened adult ASL/English learner, and I've been building a small interactive tool for a learner profile I haven't seen addressed in existing work — adults who lose their hearing post-lingually and are also non-native English speakers, learning English and a sign language in parallel.

The thing I most want your perspective on isn't the tool itself but an observation I keep coming back to. When I practise fingerspelling letter-by-letter, I find myself translating a continuous visual-spatial movement into discrete written symbols before understanding it, which feels slow and effortful. When I instead treat the whole fingerspelled word as a single continuous gesture and mimic the movement itself, retention seems noticeably faster. I understand fluent signers often read fingerspelling more holistically rather than letter-by-letter (Wilcox 1992 and subsequent work), but I haven't found much discussion around how late-deafened adult learners acquire that skill. It's changing how I'm thinking about the design of the tool.

A few things I'd genuinely value your perspective on. I'm also happy to meet online if you happen to have time; I rely heavily on live captions, so Zoom works best for me.

1. Whether the late-deafened adult learner profile matches what you see in your previous work, or whether the design unintentionally assumes pre-deafening English literacy and therefore excludes part of the population.

2. Does the observation around holistic fingerspelling sound like a meaningful learning direction, or am I potentially conflating memorisation efficiency with actual language acquisition?

3. I noticed your work on *Transforming Auslan Education in Australia*, and also saw references to fingerspelling-related work. Is there any intersection point where this project aligns conceptually, or any direction you would suggest if I continue focusing on ASL rather than Auslan?

I've attached a one-page brief with screenshots and the current state (six scenarios live, 76 signs wired). A live demo is available here: https://echozhao1998.github.io/asl-context-learning/. I can also share the source repository if useful, although I thought the demo and brief might be easier to skim initially.

I've also cc'd [Dr.Sarah](https://research.monash.edu/en/persons/sarah-goodwin/), as the initial idea partly grew out of FIT5147. I originally shared a sign-language visualisation resource on Ed, and her encouragement helped me turn that curiosity into a small project. I would value any feedback or direction you may have.

Thank you for considering it.
