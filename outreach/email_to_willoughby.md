Dear A/Prof Willoughby,

Patric [LASTNAME] suggested I reach out. I'm an MDS student at Monash and a late-deafened adult ASL/English learner, and I've been building a small interactive tool for a learner profile I haven't seen addressed in existing work — adults who lose their hearing post-lingually and are also non-native English speakers, learning English and a sign language in parallel.

The thing I most want your perspective on isn't the tool itself but an observation I keep coming back to. When I practise fingerspelling letter-by-letter — processing the 3D handshape sequence into 2D letter symbols and back — retention is slow. When I instead treat the whole fingerspelled word as a single continuous gesture and mimic the *animation* of it, retention is much faster. I understand fluent signers read fingerspelling holistically rather than letter-by-letter (Wilcox 1992 and subsequent work), but I haven't found that acquisition path discussed for late-deafened adult learners specifically. It's shaping how I'm rethinking the tool.

A few things I'd genuinely value your read on, in a 20-minute meeting if you have time:

1. Whether the late-deafened adult migrant profile matches what you see in your refugee-Deaf and adult-migrant work, or whether the design implicitly assumes pre-deafening English literacy that excludes part of the population.
2. The implications of building in ASL (because WLASL is the only freely-available scaled video dataset I've found) versus pivoting to Auslan for an Australian context — and what Auslan resources you'd recommend if I do.
3. Whether there's any intersection point between this and your *Transforming Auslan Education in Australia* project where the work could plug in.
